whatsapp-tui

WhatsApp in your terminal, driven by the keyboard like vim. It redraws in a third of a millisecond, keeps running when you close the window, and does what the official apps can't: send later, snooze chats, read without blue ticks, search by meaning, and talk to your own scripts.

BACKGROUNDone app, any number of windows

Close the window. It keeps working.

The first launch starts whatsapp-tui in the background, like tmux or herdr. Every launch after that is just a window onto it. Close the window and it stays connected: it still gets your messages, still notifies you, still sends what you scheduled. Open it again and you're exactly where you were.

q

Closing only detaches

q, :q, ctrl+c or the window's close button leave the app running. :q! or whatsapp-tui --stop quit it for real.

↺

Reopen and resume

Same chat, same scroll position, same half-written message, the same selection. Pictures redraw in the new window.

⇄

A second window takes over

Open it from another workspace and it moves there; the old window closes. It's never open twice, never logged in twice.

↑

Updates itself on the next launch

Install a new build and the next launch notices, restarts the background app on it and attaches. No stale version left behind.

0%

Costs nothing while it waits

With no window attached it stops drawing altogether. Idle, it does no work you'd notice.

No notification for the chat you're looking at, or for chats you muted on your phone (they show 🔕) unless someone mentions you. Reactions to your messages notify too ("Priya: Reacted ❤️ to: dinner at 8?").

NORMALmoving through your chats

Treat chats like an inbox.

Every chat has a next step: read it and archive it, come back later, or jump to the next one. Three keys do most of it, and they sync to your phone.

J next unread

Opens the next chat with unread messages, wherever you are.

e done

Marks the chat read, archives it, and opens the next unread one. When there's nothing left you're back at the list: inbox zero. In the archive, e brings a chat back.

U mark unread

For "deal with this later". Inside a chat it also leaves it, so opening doesn't mark it read again.

I activity

One list, across all chats: messages that @mention you, replies to your messages, and reactions to them. enter opens the message in its chat, even years back.

Mon 6 Oct 14:05 ❤️ Priya reacted in Hostel to: dinner at 8?
Mon 6 Oct 13:40 @ Ravi mentioned you in Lab: @you push the fix
Sun 5 Oct 22:12 ↩ Arjun replied in Arjun: yes, 8 works

v in the list: side by side

Opens a second chat beside the one you're in; it stays live as messages arrive. W swaps which one you write in, :only closes it.

Drafts that stay

Switch chats, close the window, restart: what you were writing is still in the box. The list shows it like WhatsApp does: Draft: see you at 8.

Find, filter, archive

Pinned chats stay on top and muted ones are marked 🔕, synced from your phone. / finds chats and contacts by name or number (archived ones too), u shows only unread, A the archive, K a chat's info and members, V a profile picture full screen.

Mentions stand out

A message that mentions you gets a red border and "@ mentioned you"; the chat gets a red @ badge. @ jumps between them.

COMMANDthings WhatsApp can't do

Send later. Snooze. Get nudged.

Because the app is always running, it can act when you're not there. Write naturally; the time is read the way people say it.

:later

Send at the right time

Write the message, esc, then :later tomorrow 9am. It goes out then, window open or not. Due while you're offline? It's sent as soon as you reconnect.

:snooze

Hide a chat until it matters

:snooze fri archives the chat; on Friday it comes back, unread, with a notification.

:nudge

"Remind me if they don't reply"

:nudge 3h. If they've answered by then, nothing happens. If not: "⏰ No reply from Priya since Mon 14:05".

Edit, forward, copy, save, delete

e edits your message (first 15 minutes), f forwards to one or several chats, y copies text or the picture, s saves media to Downloads, d deletes for you or (e) for everyone. R retries a message that didn't send.

Several at once

V starts a range, j/k grow it. Then y copies them as a transcript, f forwards them in order, d deletes them, s saves all their media.

Search that finds what you meant.

/ searches the chat you're in (its whole history), S searches every chat. Every word you type, in any order; accents ignored; capitals make it exact. A full-text index answers in 0.3 ms over 50,000 messages. With a local model installed, it also finds messages by meaning.

BENCHmeasured, not promised

Faster than the frame you're looking at.

WhatsApp's desktop app is a browser wrapper that users report at 1–2 GB of memory. whatsapp-tui's own working memory is about 5 MB. Each message bubble is drawn once and reused until it changes, so a new message, a reaction or a key press redraws in well under a millisecond.

before the speed worknowlog scale · 400-message group chat · i7-10750H

Only what changed

Rendered bubbles are cached by everything that affects how they look. Moving the selection rebuilds two bubbles, not four hundred.

Pictures as raw pixels

Images go to kitty at exactly the size they're shown, as raw pixels: no PNG encoding. At most 300 are kept, and kitty is told to forget the rest.

A tidy disk

Downloaded media is capped (1 GB by default, least recently viewed goes first); history syncs write in one transaction; search has its own index.

Quiet when idle

The cursor stops blinking when the window isn't focused, and with no window attached nothing is drawn at all.

APIyour chats, your scripts

Hook it up to anything.

The running app answers your scripts on a private local socket, and runs your hooks when something happens. Ask for chats and messages, search, schedule, subscribe to a live stream of events. Sending is off unless you allow it, and rate-limited when you do.

from the shell

# the 5 newest chats, as JSON
whatsapp-tui api chats '{"limit": 5}'

# everything about the flat, across all chats
whatsapp-tui api search '{"query": "flat address"}' | jq '.[].text'

# a live stream of mentions, one JSON line each
whatsapp-tui api subscribe '{"events": ["mention"]}'

~/.config/whatsapp-tui/hooks/on-mention (chmod +x)

#!/bin/sh
# a loud notification when someone mentions you
jq -r '"\(.message.sender_name) in \(.message.chat_name): \(.message.text)"' |
  xargs -0 notify-send -u critical "You were mentioned"

python: print every mention as it happens

import json, os, socket
s = socket.socket(socket.AF_UNIX)
s.connect(os.environ["XDG_RUNTIME_DIR"] + "/whatsapp-tui-api.sock")
f = s.makefile("rw")
f.write(json.dumps({"id": 1, "method": "subscribe"}) + "\n"); f.flush()
for line in f:
    e = json.loads(line)
    if e.get("event") == "mention":
        print(e["message"]["chat_name"], e["message"]["text"])

The full reference (every method, every field, hooks, examples) is docs/API.md in the repository.
